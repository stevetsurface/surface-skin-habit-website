Terms

A short glossary

The terms that come up most often in consultation, explained in plain language.

Hyperpigmentation
An area of skin producing excess melanin, so its colour differs from the skin around it. Melasma, sun spots and darkened acne marks all fall under this.
PIH
Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation: the dark mark left after inflammation, most often after acne. It is not scar tissue and it can fade, but it takes months.
Melasma
A symmetrical pattern of pigment, usually on the cheeks and forehead, driven by hormones and sun. It tends to recur, so it is managed over the long term rather than removed in one go.
Fitzpatrick scale
A way of grouping skin by how it reacts to sun, from type I which always burns to type VI which never does. Most Surface customers sit at types III–IV. The scale drives device settings, not whether someone qualifies.
Downtime
How long the skin looks or feels different after a procedure, before returning to normal. It is not a measure of success: plenty of effective procedures have none at all.
Atrophic scar
A depressed acne scar, formed because collagen was lost as the inflammation healed. It needs a procedure that stimulates new collagen, and almost always more than one session.
Comedone
A pore blocked with oil and dead skin cells. Open to the surface it is a blackhead, closed it is a whitehead.
Nanosecond
One billionth of a second. The length of a single q-switched laser pulse. The shorter the pulse, the more the heat stays on the target and the less spreads into surrounding skin.
Collagen
The protein that gives skin its strength and bounce. Production falls with age, and most firming procedures work by prompting the body to build new collagen.
Isotretinoin
An oral acne medication that leaves skin far more fragile in the face of procedures. Most lasers, peels and microneedling are postponed during treatment and for some time afterwards. Always tell the doctor if you are taking it or recently finished.
