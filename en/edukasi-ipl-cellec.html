TREATMENT GUIDE

IPL Cellec

Broad-spectrum light is absorbed by pigment and fine vessels, then converted to heat that makes both fade.

Session

40 minutes

Interval

every 3–4 weeks

What it feels like

Like a warm flash with a brief snap.

Recovery

Mild redness for a few hours. Pigment can look darker at first before flaking away over 5–10 days.

IN THIS GUIDEHow it worksWhat it addressesBefore treatmentAftercare

How it works

Unlike a laser, which emits one wavelength, IPL emits many at once and filters them to the target. One device can therefore be aimed at surface pigment, at redness, or at hair follicles, depending on the filter the doctor fits.

Because the light is absorbed by surface melanin, IPL works best on light to medium skin. On deeper skin tones too much energy is absorbed at the surface and the risk rises. IPL is also not a choice for melasma, as the heat can drive the pigment back more strongly.

What it addresses

  • Uneven pigmentation
  • Freckles and surface pigment
  • Redness and fine vessels
  • Sun damage
  • Unwanted hair

Discuss with your doctor

  • You are pregnant
  • Your skin has recently tanned or burnt
  • There is melasma in the treatment area
  • There is an active skin infection
  • You are taking, or recently finished, isotretinoin

Aftercare

  • Sunscreen SPF 30 or higher every day
  • Avoid direct sun for a week
  • Let the pigment flakes shed on their own, do not pick
  • Cool compress if the area feels warm

The information here is general. It is not a diagnosis, nor medical advice for your own skin. Only a doctor who examines you in person can decide what is appropriate. A doctor consultation at Surface is always free.
