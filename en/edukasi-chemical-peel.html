Chemical Peel

An acid solution loosens the bonds between dead skin cells so the top layer sheds and is replaced by newer skin.

In more depth

The acid used determines how deep it acts. Salicylic acid is oil-soluble, so it enters the pore and is used for acne. Glycolic acid works at the surface for texture and dullness. TCA works deeper and is performed only by a doctor under close supervision.

The LHALA Peel used in most Surface packages is a light peel with fine flaking. On medium and deeper skin tones, a peel that is too strong can trigger fresh pigment, so the strength is raised gradually and always on the doctor's judgement.

What it is used for

  • Acne and comedones
  • Pigment patches and darkened acne marks
  • Rough texture
  • Skin that looks tired
  • Fine lines

Light peels are safe for all skin types. Deeper peels such as TCA on deeper skin tones must be assessed by a doctor first, because of the risk of fresh pigment.

Wait if

  • You are pregnant or breastfeeding
  • You are taking, or recently finished, isotretinoin
  • There is an active skin infection, including cold sores
  • Your skin is sunburnt
  • You have had another procedure in the same area within the last four weeks

Afterwards

  • Let the skin shed on its own, do not pull it
  • A gentle cleanser and a simple moisturiser
  • Sunscreen SPF 30 or higher every day — this matters most to the result
  • Hold off on retinoids and scrubs until the flaking has finished

This is not a diagnosis

The information here is general. It is not a diagnosis, nor medical advice for your own skin. Only a doctor who examines you in person can decide what is appropriate. A doctor consultation at Surface is always free.
