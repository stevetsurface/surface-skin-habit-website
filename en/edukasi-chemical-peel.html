TREATMENT GUIDE

Chemical Peel

An acid solution loosens the bonds between dead skin cells so the top layer sheds and is replaced by newer skin.

Book a free consultation ↗Explore below ↓
Session

40 minutes

Interval

every 2–4 weeks

What it feels like

Stinging and warmth for a few minutes while the solution works.

Recovery

Depends on the ingredient, strength, and depth. A superficial peel may need about 1–7 days to settle; some very light protocols have little visible peeling. Deeper peels need longer.

How it works

The acid used determines how deep it acts. Salicylic acid is oil-soluble, so it enters the pore and is used for acne. Glycolic acid works at the surface for texture and dullness. TCA works deeper and is performed only by a doctor under close supervision.

The LHALA Peel used in most Surface packages is a light peel with fine flaking. On medium and deeper skin tones, a peel that is too strong can trigger fresh pigment, so the strength is raised gradually and always on the doctor's judgement.

What it addresses

  • Acne and comedones
  • Pigment patches and darkened acne marks
  • Rough texture
  • Skin that looks tired
  • Fine lines

Discuss with your doctor

  • You are pregnant or breastfeeding
  • You are taking, or recently finished, isotretinoin
  • There is an active skin infection, including cold sores
  • Your skin is sunburnt
  • You have had another procedure in the same area within the last four weeks

Aftercare

  • Let the skin shed on its own, do not pull it
  • A gentle cleanser and a simple moisturiser
  • Sunscreen SPF 30 or higher every day — this matters most to the result
  • Hold off on retinoids and scrubs until the flaking has finished

The information here is general. It is not a diagnosis, nor medical advice for your own skin. Only a doctor who examines you in person can decide what is appropriate. A doctor consultation at Surface is always free.

A LITTLE PREPARATION HELPS

Recovery, without the guesswork.

Most expected reactions are temporary. Knowing what is usual—and what needs a call—helps you plan with confidence.

TIME TO PLAN

Downtime

Depends on the ingredient, strength, and depth. A superficial peel may need about 1–7 days to settle; some very light protocols have little visible peeling. Deeper peels need longer.

OFTEN TEMPORARY

What you may notice

Stinging during treatment, followed by temporary redness, tightness, dryness, or flaking.

Potential risks and side effects

Persistent pigment changes, infection or a cold-sore flare, and scarring can occur. Risk rises with deeper treatment and varies with skin type.

Before your treatment

Discuss sun exposure, cold sores, medicines, and recent treatments. Do not pick peeling skin; follow the prescribed skin care and sun protection.

When to contact us

Contact the clinic if discomfort is increasing, redness is spreading, blisters appear, or recovery is different from what was explained. Seek urgent medical care for vision changes, breathing or swallowing difficulty, or severe symptoms.
